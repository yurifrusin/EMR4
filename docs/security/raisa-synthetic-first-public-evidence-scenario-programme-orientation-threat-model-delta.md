## Threats and controls

### Source-authority laundering

Threat: vendor marketing, a fictional portrayal, model output or an observed
practice pattern is silently promoted into a safety or clinical oracle.

Control: closed source classes and an oracle-eligibility rule. Vendor and
fiction classes may generate candidate dimensions only. Model output cannot
author or adjudicate an oracle. Private observations may calibrate frequency
only after a separate accepted privacy review.

### Copyright-derived scenario reproduction

Threat: guideline tables, vendor manuals, dialogue, characters, plots or
distinctive fictional scenes are copied or lightly renamed.

Control: record premise-level citations and abstract tension atoms only;
compose rather than adapt; prohibit dialogue, transcripts, screenshots and
recognisable narrative fingerprints; retain source-specific rights posture.

### Synthetic-label confusion

Threat: de-identified observed sequences are called synthetic, obscuring their
privacy risk and provenance.

Control: closed evidence labels distinguish wholly authored, privately
calibrated generated, de-identified observed and raw private material. Labels
cannot be promoted by an execution result.

### Temporal fingerprint and re-identification

Threat: exact dates, times, rare sequences, family groupings, practitioner
rhythms or community events re-identify a person, practice or day after direct
identifiers are removed.

Control: no diary access in this tranche. A later local-only review must assess
relative-time transformation, date shifting, free-text removal, direct-
identifier removal, rare-cell/sequence suppression, bucketing, aggregate motif
generation and adversarial linkage/membership attacks. No observed day-level
sequence may enter development fixtures or provider prompts by default.

### False event-time precision

Threat: a snapshot filename is treated as the moment an appointment changed.

Control: represent each inferred event with an interval bounded by adjacent
snapshot observation times and preserve polling gaps and ambiguous multi-change
intervals.

### Product/runtime leakage

Threat: a research scenario or provider response is connected to production
commands, credentials, networks or real services.

Control: orientation remains documentation/evidence-only. The next envelope
tranche is provider-free and unmounted. Provider-live use requires a later
exact plan, synthetic packet, cost/call ledger, no-write broker and
preexecution receipt.

### Duplicate scenario engines

Threat: a new universal schema reimplements and diverges from the accepted
ReceptionScenarioSpec and YAML replay laboratory.

Control: build a traceability envelope and explicit legacy bindings; domain
payloads remain owned by their existing contracts.

### Coverage theatre

Threat: a large scenario count is mistaken for safety, realism or completeness.

Control: measure declared denominators, requirement/hazard/state coverage,
pairwise and risk-selected higher-order interactions, metamorphic consistency,
mutation-test rejection and repeated-run reliability. Synthetic evidence does
not establish real-world efficacy.

### Protected-evidence contamination

Threat: protected holdouts or support files influence development taxonomy or
generated scenarios.

Control: do not enumerate, search, open, hash, sample or infer any protected
surface. Future protected evaluation receives only opaque references and
aggregate typed results.

## Residual risk

Public-source scenarios can be broad and clinically grounded but cannot prove
ecological validity, local practice fit, current prevalence, clinical benefit
or production safety. Aggregate-calibrated synthetic data may reduce but cannot
make privacy risk literally zero. Those limitations must remain explicit in
every claim and closeout.
